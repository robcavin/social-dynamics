BASE MODEL ARCHITECTURE NOTES
=============================

Key finding: The spatial memory field described in Richard's PDF (Section 8)
is NOT yet implemented in the 3D codebase. The 3D sim has:
- Positions: pos (n, 3) in [0, SPACE)^3 where SPACE=1.0
- Preferences: prefs (n, k) in [-1, 1]
- Direction memory: dir_matrix (n, k, 3) — EMA of movement directions
- Neighbor finding: KNN or radius-based on spatial positions
- Physics: per-dimension best-neighbor or inner-product-average
- Social learning: prefs shift toward neighbor mean (line 740)
- Trail rendering: visual only, not used in physics

The simulation loop (step()) does:
1. Find neighbors (KNN/radius on spatial positions)
2. Physics: compute movement from preference-weighted neighbor directions
3. Position update: pos = (pos + step_size * movement) % SPACE
4. Social learning: prefs blend toward neighbor mean
5. Clip prefs to [-1, 1]

HOOK POINTS for Knowledge Manifold:
- After step 3 (position update): deposit knowledge at new positions
- Between step 1 and 2: read knowledge heights, compute rewards
- In step 4 (social learning): weight by reward
- New: visionary spatial drift added to movement in step 3

The 3D domain is [0,1)^3 but we can use (x,y) as the skill plane
and z as the vertical axis for visualization.

CRITICAL: The base model uses 3D positions. For the knowledge manifold,
the "skill space" is naturally the (x,y) plane of the spatial domain.
The z-axis can represent the knowledge height for visualization.
This means particles literally "walk on the mountain" — their z-position
IS their knowledge height.
